# Jeff Electric video storyteller

Design the viewing experience before choosing effects. Read [the shot and edit worksheet](references/shot-and-edit.md), the [creative review](../jeff-electric-creative-director/references/creative-review.md), and the relevant installed HyperFrames core/media-use references before production.

## Establish what can be shown

Inspect source media directly: motion, framing, usable action, focus/resolution, audio, and sensitive details. Verify whether images belong to the same scene or job before proposing a transformation. State when a file called “video” is actually a still montage. Distinguish installed equipment from a verified operational test or completed inspection.

Match the format to the material. A reveal needs a visible change. A skit needs a performable situation and reaction. A process piece needs action. A personal story needs an authentic voice or performance. Still photography can support a deliberate photo story, but arbitrary zooms and swooshes do not supply missing action. Do not claim real job evidence from generated footage.

## Build the sequence

Specify the first frame, the meaningful change or question, and the payoff. Put the most compelling supported moment early. Give every shot a narrative or perceptual purpose; remove shots included only to fill a duration. Choose the runtime from what needs to be read, seen, or heard.

Write a shot-linked script with timing, framing, actual on-screen words, performance direction, sound, and the exact asset or missing shot. Keep necessary subjects and words clear of platform UI. Reframe for each aspect ratio; do not stretch. A high-resolution export does not prove that source footage is detailed or well composed.

For humor, direct credible reactions, timing, and contrast between expectations and reality. Do not rely on stock “POV” captions, dancing without a premise, exaggerated incompetence, unsafe electrical acts, or mocking a customer. Brand presence should fit the content; an organic moment does not always need an opening logo or closing sales panel.

## Direct sound deliberately

Decide what the viewer hears before rendering: original speech, useful natural sound, cleared music, subtle effects, or an intentional quiet passage. Sound-off understanding does not require a silent export. A silent version is a creative choice, not an automatic fallback when audio is inconvenient.

Load the installed media-use skill for media operations and audio. Establish rights before using a song or clip, and do not incur paid generation without authorization. Designed sound effects must not be represented as recorded site audio. Do not claim an AI voice is an actual employee or customer. A useful capture brief can name one line to record, the action needed, and the sound that makes the moment work.

## Review the actual result

Before rendering substantially, resolve creative blockers and assess the treatment against the available assets. After rendering, inspect actual exported frames and transitions at phone size; compare the delivered payoff with the opening promise. Review with sound and muted where tools permit. Audio-stream metadata does not establish intelligibility, synchronization, or musical fit; report what was actually heard versus measured.

Fix the highest-impact creative defect before polishing fonts or adding effects. Keep production, rights, and creative findings separate. If the tools can only finish a technically valid but visibly inferior substitute, state the limitation and preserve a useful treatment/shot list rather than marketing the substitute as a strong finished creative.
