# Creative review separate from export QA

Give a reviewer the intended audience/objective, treatment, source-media observations, and relevant owner feedback. Ask for concrete observations, not an enthusiasm score. If a key reference cannot be watched, do not invent its pacing or performance.

For each item, name the exact shot/line/time that supports it, or the specific revision needed:

1. **Viewer reason:** What does a homeowner receive for watching? A company wanting attention is not a viewer benefit.
2. **Opening promise:** Does the opening image and line accurately set up what the video delivers?
3. **Payoff:** Identify the reveal, answer, reaction, result, or useful insight. Is it worth the setup?
4. **Copy and performance:** Could a real person say this naturally? Does the humor arise from the situation rather than a generic caption?
5. **Media fit:** Do the actual assets support the chosen mechanism and claims at usable quality?
6. **Pacing and sound:** Does each cut, hold, caption, and sound have a job? Is the sequence understandable muted and effective with its intended sound?
7. **Trust:** Are facts, privacy, job relationships, rights, and limitations handled accurately?
8. **Taste fit:** Does it avoid the owner's explicit rejected directions? What is materially different about this attempt?

Use three dispositions:

- **Ready for production/review:** The concept is supported and no unresolved blocker prevents the next authorized stage. This is not a forecast of audience performance.
- **Revise:** Give the highest-impact concrete change and affected moment. Avoid vague “make it punchier” feedback.
- **Needs assets or facts:** Name the missing item and smallest way to obtain it. Do not render an inferior substitute to make the workflow look complete.

Misleading claims, invented job evidence, unlicensed assets, an undelivered hook, or absent required footage block production of that concept. Do not force approval by averaging these into a numeric score. A deliberate still-photo format can pass only if its editorial purpose and execution support the brief; stills are not categorically bad.

Before delivery, inspect the actual exported sequence at phone size. Check visible text, scene transitions, subject framing, and the promised payoff. Compare the result to the treatment, not just the code. State anything unreviewed. Do not label content “great,” “viral,” “ad-approved,” or “ready to win” because it passed file checks.
