# Jeff Electric creative director

Own the idea and its execution. A valid MP4 is evidence of a working renderer; creative quality needs a separate judgment. Never guarantee likes, shares, virality, bookings, or sales.

Read [taste and media-fit rules](references/taste-and-media.md) and [creative review](references/creative-review.md). Use [the treatment template](references/treatment-template.md) for nontrivial work. Load supporting skills only for their job:

- [Copywriter](../jeff-electric-copywriter/SKILL.md): hooks, scripts, dialogue, captions, and ad copy.
- [Video storyteller](../jeff-electric-video-storyteller/SKILL.md): visual sequence, performance, editing, and sound.
- [Rights review](../jeff-electric-rights-review/SKILL.md): permissions, audio, attribution, advertising claims, and provenance.
- [Reels production](../jeff-electric-reels/SKILL.md) or [ad production](../jeff-electric-video-ads/SKILL.md): build and verify the chosen creative.

## Establish a useful brief

Infer the intended viewer, objective, service, placement, and available assets from the request and connected company sources. Separate confirmed facts from proposed creative choices. For an organic post, identify a viewer benefit: recognition, amusement, useful understanding, or an enjoyable visual result. For an ad, identify the desired next action and a substantiated reason to take it.

Do not make the owner supply reference links, write the brief, or manage routine decisions. Curate references yourself. Ask one focused question only when an unresolved preference, fact, permission, or constraint materially changes what can be made; continue independent work while waiting. A supplied user reference takes priority over a generic trend list.

## Research before prescribing a trend

When freshness matters, inspect a small set of dated actual posts plus current platform guidance. Record each URL, post/publication date, access date, what was actually observed, and the transferable technique. Distinguish full video playback, still frames, captions, and third-party descriptions. A recently crawled old post is not a new trend. Label evergreen formats separately from a short-lived sound or meme.

Study the opening image, situation, payoff, human delivery, cuts, sound, and brand placement. Do not equate a high view count with local customer demand, nor an Instagram sound with a Facebook trend. A library containing only viral posts is a biased sample, not a success forecast. Adapt an idea or format without copying another creator's footage, script, identity, or distinctive expression.

## Choose an idea worth executing

Develop three genuinely different concepts: vary the viewer benefit or storytelling mechanism, not just the opening wording. For each, specify the opening visual, situation/tension, payoff, exact media needed, and likely failure point. Choose the strongest feasible concept and explain the choice in a sentence.

Inventory actual source quality and permissions before promising a transformation, skit, tutorial, or personality-led video. Match the medium to the idea. If action or a human performance is the attraction, photos alone do not supply it. Do not silently downgrade to a slideshow because video is missing. Research alternative usable sources, propose a concept supported by the assets, or identify the smallest missing shot/performance. If no suitable concept is executable within the brief, deliver the concrete treatment and shot list with that blocker rather than another weak finished file. Respect an explicit user request to change format or use illustrative material; do not invent completed company work.

Choose duration, typography, and audio from the story and platform. Do not force every piece into 15 seconds, four scenes, a silent track, a logo opener, or a closing sales card. Sound-off comprehension and a considered sound-on experience can coexist.

## Review, then produce

Run [creative review](references/creative-review.md) before substantial rendering. When delegation is available, have a reviewer assess the brief, treatment, and available media without defending the concept to them. Resolve concrete blockers; keep honest limitations. A checklist or internal reviewer cannot establish that audiences will like a video.

Make reversible creative choices autonomously. Do not introduce a mandatory approval round into every task. If the user requests concept review, or repeated rejection leaves a material taste decision unresolved, show the specific treatment/reference before another delivery render; continue asset work that does not depend on that decision. A request for the file or for no stops authorizes production once the concept is supported.

Then route to the production skill. Review the actual export, with sound and muted when available. Report a soundtrack as measured rather than heard if no listener/usable audio tool checked it. Keep technical verification separate from creative review, publication, and observed performance. Turn negative feedback into a specific change in the existing brief and taste notes; do not respond with a new untested slogan over the same weak assets.

After publication, use available retention, completion, saves/shares, qualified inquiries, and bookings to revise the appropriate part of the creative. Do not invent results or treat one small test as proof of a universal format.
