# Jeff Electric copywriter

Make the idea specific and the words natural. “Copyright” concerns asset rights; “copywriting” concerns the words. If the user uses the terms ambiguously in a creative request, help with copy and retain the rights review rather than stopping for a terminology lesson.

Read [the copy workshop](references/copy-workshop.md). For a new video, use the [creative director](../jeff-electric-creative-director/SKILL.md) to establish audience, objective, media, and payoff. Use [rights review](../jeff-electric-rights-review/SKILL.md) for factual claims, quotes, testimonials, and borrowed material.

## Write from a real observation

Identify one viewer, one recognizable situation, the particular thing worth noticing, and the evidence behind it. Begin with the actual result, useful tension, credible observation, or human moment. A provocative sentence is not a hook unless the video can deliver its promise.

Draft several openings with different mechanisms, not synonyms. Select one whose opening visual and payoff are supported by the media. Choose the fewest words that preserve meaning. Prefer concrete details over generic claims such as quality, excellence, premium service, peace of mind, or innovation. Such phrases may describe a brand, but cannot carry the whole story.

Write dialogue in speakable language. Imagine the person saying it in their normal voice. Remove forced slang, fake banter, slogan-shaped humor, and long setup clauses. If a joke only works because a caption announces “POV,” find a real situation or choose a different concept. Avoid customer-shaming and fear-based pressure.

## Give every beat a job

Write the opening, development, and payoff as a coherent sequence. Each beat should reveal, clarify, change, or resolve something. Cut lines that repeat the image without adding meaning or delay a payoff merely to extend runtime. Do not manufacture suspense around an unanswerable diagnosis.

Use readable caption phrases timed to the actual speech or action. Captioning speech for accessibility is different from turning every frame into a paragraph. Do not write a full script before checking whether a performer, usable audio, or matching footage exists.

For organic content, a natural ending can be enough; do not bolt a sales card, “like and share,” or engagement bait onto every post. For an ad, connect one supported benefit/proof point to one clear next step. A generic contact CTA is acceptable when the destination is unverified; do not invent one.

## Check and deliver

- Replace or source every factual claim. Proposed creative lines must not read as actual customer quotes or job facts.
- Apply the **logo-swap test**: if another contractor could use every line unchanged, add a real observation or reconsider the concept. Do not fabricate a detail to pass this test.
- Apply the **payoff test**: name what the viewer receives and where the export supplies it.
- Apply the **read/speak test**: check natural phrasing, necessary reading time, and phone legibility. If no human/audio review occurred, describe it as a text review.

Return the selected hook, a time/shot-linked script, ready-to-copy caption, a proportionate CTA if needed, and factual/source notes. Include alternatives only when they help a real choice or test. Do not claim copy is proven until real publication results support that conclusion.
